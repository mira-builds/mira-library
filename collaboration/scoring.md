# Shelf sufficiency and completeness

Ratings are ordinal judgments for a named target, purpose and languages. They
are not percentages of everything an author wrote. Public candidate proposals
do not assign authoritative ratings.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1–6 | Insufficient; identify the material shortfall for the purpose. |
| 7 | A representative core is verified, usable and supported by an index, provisional profile and reconciled assessment. |
| 8 | Broad usable coverage beyond that core. |
| 9 | Near complete, with enumerated bounded gaps nonessential to the purpose. |
| 10 | Every declared component/language and all seven completion gates pass. |

The gates are scope, work/edition extent, language coverage, integrity/usability,
index, source-grounded profile and reconciliation. Missing evidence remains
unassessed; it is not a fabricated failure. File counts, hashes and headings do
not prove text completeness or full reading. Source numbering can reflect an
edition choice, and newer upstream readings require judgment rather than
automatic replacement. Core performs final admission and assessment under its
governing standard; private evidence is not automatically published.
