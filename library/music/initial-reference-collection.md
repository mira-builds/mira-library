# Music Initial Reference Collection

## Purpose and disposition

This proposed first collection gives the Music section a scholarly foundation across reference, theory, scores, œuvre catalogues, biography, documents, criticism, and performance. It follows the [Music architecture](architecture.md) and its [Debussy example](debussy-catalogue-example.md). Selection rationales are curatorial judgments, not claims that these sources are infallible or have been fully read.

There are 18 proposed entries: some are individual books, others are edition pairs or institutional resources. This is not a count of volumes or admitted authorities. Each entry has a stable planning ID; these are not production registry IDs. All remain `proposed`, with body research incomplete. Public reference pages and selected publisher materials were checked on 2026-10-01; no source bodies were downloaded or admitted, no purchases or subscriptions were made, and existing Library holdings were not audited.

Priority 1 establishes the first working foundation; priority 2 adds depth and contrasting perspectives. Priority is not permission to acquire. An inspected catalogue page establishes a reference route, not access to the whole book or score. Where only discovery evidence was available, that limit is explicit.

A broad discovery repository can help locate them but does not replace the edition-level source record.

## First working group and growth gaps

The ten priority-1 entries are 01, 03, 04, 07, 09, 10, 11, 13, 14, and 15. Together they connect a general reference, two analytical textbooks, Mozart catalogue/scores/documents, a Debussy biography and edition pair, and German/French criticism. Priority-2 entries are 02, 05, 06, 08, 12, 16, 17, and 18. These are proposed inspection groups, not acquisitions already in progress.

The collection is deliberately initial. Its gaps include:

- A broad music-history survey and a basic harmony/counterpoint sequence.
- Complete catalogue and score coverage for additional major composers, including Haydn, Schubert, Chopin, Brahms, Wagner, Mahler, and major twentieth-century figures.
- Italian, Russian, Spanish-language original criticism and criticism outside Western Europe and the United States.
- Women as composers, critics, performers, and scholars; the present critical quartet is entirely male.
- Earlier repertories, opera and vocal-text scholarship, sacred music, and traditions beyond the initial classical focus.
- A stronger recording-history reference beyond Mozart and explicit original/translation pairs for criticism.

These gaps should shape the next roster expansion; they are not claims that this collection represents the entire field. Existing playlist preferences do not exclude composers from the Library.
