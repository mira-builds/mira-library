# Music — composer coverage roster

Status: local collection-development proposal, 1 October 2026. No sources acquired, holdings audited, or registry records created by this document.

Companion documents: [architecture](architecture.md), [initial reference collection](initial-reference-collection.md), [catalogue authorities for the first twelve composers](catalogue-authorities.md), and [Debussy catalogue example](debussy-catalogue-example.md).

## Purpose and scope

Build a Music shelf that can support informed listening, interpretation, study, and Mira Music curation. For each composer below, the eventual target is a catalogue of the full known œuvre and documented score coverage. Starting with selected works is a work sequence, not a permanent limit on coverage.

This initial roster contains 48 composers. It is a curatorial proposal, not a ranking of greatness or a claim to represent all classical music. The groups organize work; composers may move forward when a concrete reading, listening, or composition need arises. Fauré and Grieg belong here: exclusions from one playlist do not exclude a composer from the Library. The three-composer cover limit applies to playlists, not this shelf.

The study emphases below are proposed starting points, not catalogue boundaries. Coverage remains proposed for all rows. Catalogue authority routes for Group 1 are documented in the companion authority map, including the unresolved Ravel revision choice and Haydn's need for attribution corrections. Full-body access, work-level reconciliation, and holdings checks remain open.

## What full coverage means

For every composer, identify a scholarly catalogue or explicitly reconciled set of authorities. Record its author/editor, edition or revision date, scope, identifiers, and known limitations. Where no single comprehensive authority exists, retain that gap rather than substituting an unqualified web list.

Separate these coverage measures:

1. **Catalogue coverage:** all entries in the declared authority are represented or explicitly accounted for. Distinguish authenticated, attributed/disputed, rejected, lost, unfinished, and fragmentary works according to that authority. Preserve its uncertainty.
2. **Version coverage:** link revisions, arrangements, orchestrations, alternate instrumentations, and collaborative contributions without silently counting them as independent works or discarding them.
3. **Score coverage:** for surviving in-scope music, identify an inspectable score source and its edition, completeness, instrumentation, editorial apparatus, and access conditions. A keyboard reduction is not a full orchestral score; parts and full scores are separate objects.
4. **Local holdings:** record what is actually held, where, in which format, and whether inspected. A catalogue link or publisher listing is not a holding. Unavailable or lost scores remain explicit exceptions.

Never state “complete” without naming the authority, revision, denominator, and treatment of exceptions. Report catalogue and score coverage separately; neither demonstrates that the music has been read or heard.

Each composer should also have a research companion set: a scholarly biography; available letters or other primary documents; analytical scholarship; criticism in relevant original languages and identified translations; and performance or recording references. These supplement the œuvre record rather than replace it. Musical criticism belongs to its author and publication context, with links to the composers and works it discusses.

## Authority and source routes

The [initial reference collection](initial-reference-collection.md) identifies starting authorities for Bach, Mozart, and Beethoven, plus a bounded Debussy example. Its bibliographical discoveries are not evidence of complete composer coverage.

- [Henle's thematic-indices directory](https://www.henle.de/Thematic-Indices/) is a publisher discovery route for candidate catalogue editions. Inspect the exact volume and scope before selecting it as an authority; a directory listing alone does not establish completeness.
- [RISM](https://rism.info/service/faq.html) locates historical musical sources and their holding institutions. Its coverage is incomplete, it does not document lost sources, and a record does not guarantee a digital copy. Use it alongside composer catalogues and institutional inventories, not as the denominator for the full œuvre.
- For each composer, investigate the relevant scholarly complete edition, composer society or archive, institutional catalogue, and publisher. These remain research routes until the exact resource is verified.

The two general routes above were checked on 1 October 2026. The roster and study priorities are editorial proposals, not rankings derived from those sources.

## Work sequence and remaining gaps

First map catalogue authorities for Group 1, reusing the existing reference discoveries. For each composer, record the selected authority or unresolved candidates, identifier systems, known scope exclusions, and a score-source route. Check existing holdings before acquisition proposals. In subsequent batches, interleave the other groups rather than trying to finish every score for one group first.

Maintain the full-œuvre target while beginning score inspection with a bounded set that exercises different source problems: vocal text, orchestral versus reduced score, revisions, attribution, and electronic or nonstandard notation. Source acquisition and admission follow the Library Import contract separately.

This is an initial 48-composer scope, not a closed canon. Material omissions for later consideration include Byrd and Tallis; Lully, Rameau, Domenico Scarlatti, and C. P. E. Bach; Rossini and Puccini; Mussorgsky and Rimsky-Korsakov; Janáček, Nielsen, and Scriabin; Berg, Webern, and Ligeti; and wider living-composer and geographic coverage. Traditions beyond this primarily European classical lineage require their own thoughtful coverage plan, not a claim that this roster already represents them.

The planning IDs in this file are local reference labels, not admitted Library registry identifiers. This document creates no schema support, source-body holdings, purchases, or publication state.
